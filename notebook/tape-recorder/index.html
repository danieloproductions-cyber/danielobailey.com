The Tape Recorder

The tape recorder evokes a game-like quality I explored last night with everyone here, resembling the Dick phone. I ask questions so they pour themselves out, not to me but to the device, and I always ensure they can destroy the tape, which offers a sense of liberty and freedom. Foret is never to be tricked; you cannot deceive someone if you destroy the cheap Type I cassette before them when they request its destruction.

If you ignore them at the phone, their words remain forever.

An anxiety from the tongue reaches my stomach and makes me feel uneasy about contributing to this, since I want to speak and connect with you, yet is it taken away?

It makes me think I am smarter than I am; another effect is that it leads everyone to believe this; a third is that it removes experiences. If you consider it, it is odd that a tape recorder (or ChatGPT, or AI) might make you more interesting and easier to connect with over a month of use than holding a cellphone.

All the cellphone does is distract you and clog your mind with what-ifs, calls, events, or locations; before you know it, you are 45 years old and life has passed in this goddamn technology shock of a sale. I will not jump to any answer yet while I ponder this, but I will write a lot. I wrote at least 35 pages last night in a fine street of organized anger and then lashed Margaret with every sort of uppercut you could imagine in the nicest, most diplomatic sense, because I am a gentleman.
